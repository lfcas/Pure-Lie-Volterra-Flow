# Pure Lie-Volterra Flow: Continuum Dynamics and Mechanistic Interpretability on Hyperspherical Manifolds

This repository contains standalone Jupyter notebooks implementing and analyzing the **Pure Lie-Volterra Flow (PLVF)**, a differentiable sequence architecture formulated on principles of continuum mechanics, Hamiltonian transport, and integral operators derived from the **Local Field Learning (LFL)** framework.

Rather than processing token sequences in unconstrained Euclidean vector spaces ($\mathbb{R}^d$) via pairwise softmax attention, PLVF models sequence computation as continuous geodesic integration over the compact hypersphere $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$. State updates are governed by rank-2 antisymmetric generators belonging to the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(d)$, driven by a causal Volterra-Fredholm integral operator with an orthogonal Chebyshev polynomial memory basis.

The repository investigates whether imposing compact spherical manifolds and continuous Lie-algebraic generators naturally induces structured algebraic representations (such as discrete Fourier modes, subgroup quotients, and conjugacy classes) across formal, algebraic, and boolean tasks.

## Core Mathematical Formulations

### 1. Hyperspherical Manifold and Closed-Form Geodesic Transport

All hidden states and embedding vectors are constrained to the compact unit hypersphere $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$:

$$
\Pi_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(x) = \frac{x}{\|x\|_2 + \epsilon}
$$

State transitions along tangent vectors $v \in T_x \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ are executed via the closed-form Riemannian exponential map $\mathrm{Exp}_x(v): T_x \mathbb{S}^{d-1} \to \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$, bypassing Euclidean residual additions:

$$
\mathrm{Exp}_x(v) = \cos\left(\min(\|v\|_2, \theta_{\max})\right) x + \mathrm{sinc}\left(\min(\|v\|_2, \theta_{\max})\right) v
$$

where numerical stability around $\|v\|_2 \to 0$ is preserved via quadratic regularization:

$$
\mathrm{sinc}(\|v\|_2) = \frac{\sin(\|v\|_2)}{\sqrt{\|v\|_2^2 + \epsilon^2}}
$$

### 2. Intrinsic Geodesic Skip Connections vs. Euclidean Residuals

Standard sequence architectures rely on additive Euclidean residual bypasses:

$$
x_{t+1} = x_t + f(x_t)
$$

Additive bypasses distort activation norms, dilate vector magnitudes, and push representations outside bounded manifolds, necessitating heuristic normalizations (LayerNorm, RMSNorm). 

PLVF eliminates additive skip connections in favor of the Riemannian Exponential Map. Decomposing the exponential map reveals an intrinsic geodesic skip mechanism:
* **Identity Preservation Term ($\cos(\|v\|_2) x$):** As the tangent force vanishes ($\|v\|_2 \to 0$), $\cos(\|v\|_2) \to 1$, preserving state identity along the manifold geodesic.
* **Tangent Perturbation Term ($\mathrm{sinc}(\|v\|_2) v$):** Introduces orthogonal rotational updates generated by the Lie algebra without perturbing radial scale.

Because $\langle x, v \rangle \equiv 0$, the manifold state satisfies $\|x_{t+1}\|_2 \equiv 1.0$ identically across all layers, providing a clean gradient path through depth without norm explosion.

### 3. Lie Algebra Generator in $\mathfrak{so}(d)$

Tangent forces are synthesized through the canonical action of rank-2 antisymmetric bivectors. Given local interaction fields $u, v \in \mathbb{R}^d$, the operator $\Omega = u \wedge v \in \mathfrak{so}(d)$ acts directly on the current manifold state $x$:

$$
\Omega(x) = (u v^T - v u^T) x = (v^T x) u - (u^T x) v
$$

This construction satisfies the geometric orthogonality condition identically:

$$
\langle x, \, \Omega(x) \rangle = (v^T x)(x^T u) - (u^T x)(x^T v) \equiv 0
$$

The resulting vector field is strictly tangent to $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ by construction, generating norm-preserving rotations without requiring numerical projection steps.

### 4. Causal Volterra-Fredholm Memory Operator

Temporal context aggregation replaces pairwise dot-product attention matrices with a continuous second-kind integral operator evaluated over an orthogonal Chebyshev polynomial basis:

$$
h(t) = \int_0^t \mathcal{K}(t, s) \, j(s) \, ds
$$

Discretized causally across sequence horizon $T$ using $M$ harmonic modes:

1. **Gauss-Chebyshev Quadrature**: Temporal coordinates $t \in [0, T-1]$ are mapped to Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind $T_m(x)$, weighted by the singular density $w(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x(1 - x) + \epsilon}}$.
2. **Multiscale Viscoelastic Decay**: Modes relax across temporal intervals via learnable dissipation rates $\gamma_m \in [10^{-4}, 2.0]$:

   $$
   \mathcal{K}(t, s) = \sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m \, \Phi_m(t) \, \Psi_m(s) \, \exp\left(-\gamma_m (t - s)\right) \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{s \le t\}}
   $$

3. **Second-Order Volterra Coupling**: The memory field $h(t)$ interacts non-linearly with the instantaneous state $x(t)$ via quadratic gating:

   $$
   z(t) = \sigma(W_u x(t)) \odot \sigma(W_v h(t)), \quad F(t) = W_{\text{proj}} z(t)
   $$

### 5. Thermal Phase Bounding

Classification logits are computed directly as Riemannian distance metrics (cosine similarities) against class representations on $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$, scaled by an inverse temperature parameter $\tau$:

$$
\text{Logits}_c = \tau \cdot \langle x_{\text{final}}, \, \bar{w}_c \rangle = \tau \cos(\theta_c)
$$

$$
\mathrm{tau} = \mathrm{clip}(\exp(\log \mathrm{tau}), \, \mathrm{tau}_{\mathrm{min}}, \, \mathrm{tau}_{\mathrm{max}})
$$

Thermal bounding prevents logit saturation, bounds decision entropy, and stabilizes gradient backpropagation through the manifold.

## Mechanistic Interpretability Records (`02_plvf_mechanistic_visualizations.ipynb`)

Notebook `02` extracts the internal geometries, projection weights, and activation tensors from converged PLVF checkpoints:

### 1. Modular Addition: Torus $\mathbb{T}^4 \subset \mathbb{S}^{127}$ & Fourier Circuits
* **Fourier Sparsity**: Decomposing embedding variance across all 48 possible frequencies reveals concentration in four dominant modes: $\omega \in \{2, 17, 29, 40\}$. In representative runs, $\omega = 29$ captures $14.4\%$ FVE, $\omega = 2$ captures $12.3\%$, $\omega = 17$ captures $12.3\%$, and $\omega = 40$ captures $11.4\%$, with background modes remaining near zero.
* **Geodesic Circles & Linearity**: Projections onto dominant frequency subplanes form closed $\mathrm{SO}(2)$ circles. Empirical rotation angles match theoretical angles with high linearity ($y = x$ along $[0^\circ, 360^\circ]$).
* **$\mathbb{T}^4$ Torus Matrix**: A $4 \times 4$ projection matrix of the dominant modes displays pure circular trajectories on the diagonal and clean Lissajous curves off-diagonal (parabolic, figure-eight, and toroidal braids).
* **Neuron Probing**: 1D sweeps ($a=0$, sweeping $b \in [0, 96]$) reveal pure sinusoidal tuning matching theoretical frequencies. 2D interaction grids $(a \times b)$ display clear $-45^\circ$ diagonal bands, reflecting $(a + b) \pmod p$ modular addition.

### 2. Modular Multiplication: Discrete Logarithm & $\mathbb{Z}_6$ Hexagonal Quotient
* **Linear vs. Discrete Log Ordering**: Plotting non-zero residues $k \in [1, 96]$ in natural integer order yields an irregular pattern jumping between clusters. Reordering residues by powers of the primitive root $g = 5$ ($x = 5^k \pmod{97}$) collapses the representation into a structured orbit.
* **Subgroup Homomorphism**: Probing the dominant mode ($\omega = 16$) reveals factoring of the multiplicative group $\mathbb{Z}_{96}^\times$ into the cyclic quotient $\mathbb{Z}_6$ ($96 / 16 = 6$). The 96 non-zero residues organize into a **regular hexagon** on the hypersphere, with 16 elements clustered at each vertex.

### 3. Formal Language Dyck-1: Continuous Polar Winding Counter
* **Phase Trajectories**: Tracking internal state trajectories $x(t)$ across temporal steps $t \in [0, 12]$ in 2D PCA demonstrates that valid balanced sequences remain bounded within an equatorial disk and return strictly to the origin ($\theta \to 0$), while invalid sequences diverge into outer regions.
* **Winding Calibration**: Unwrapping the continuous geodesic phase angle $\theta(t) = \mathrm{unwrap}(\mathrm{atan2}(\text{PC}_2, \text{PC}_1))$ reveals a monotonic relationship with theoretical stack depth, indicating that the causal Volterra operator functions as a continuous phase-space integrator.

### 4. Permutations $S_5$: Frobenius Conjugacy Class Shells
* **Non-Abelian Invariant Structure**: 2D PCA projections of the 120 permutation representations do not collapse into 1D circles. Instead, they organize into concentric shells corresponding to the 7 conjugacy classes of $S_5$ ($1^5, 2\cdot 1^3, 2^2\cdot 1, 3\cdot 1^2, 3\cdot 2, 4\cdot 1, 5$).
* **Trace Preservation**: Conjugate permutations $\sigma$ and $g \sigma g^{-1}$ inhabit shared radial shells, reflecting the invariance of the character $\chi(\sigma) = \mathrm{tr}(\sigma)$.

### 5. Sparse Parity: Walsh-Hadamard Monomial Isolation
* **Support Recovery**: Evaluating the $L_2$ norms of input projection weights $\|W_{\text{in}}[i]\|_2$ across all $N=20$ bits shows that spurious bits remain bounded near a uniform baseline ($\approx 2.2$).
* **Dirac Deltas**: The three active bits ($\text{Target Bits} = [2, 7, 11]$) emerge as distinct peaks with norms exceeding $3.0$ ($3.03, 3.04, 3.30$), isolating the hidden parity monomial on $\{-1, 1\}^3$.

### 6. Random Lookup Hash: Isotropic White Noise Control
* **Spectral Uniformity**: Evaluating Fourier FVE (%) across all 31 frequencies for the tabular hash control reveals a flat distribution centered around the theoretical white noise baseline:
  $$\text{Baseline} = \frac{100\%}{31} \approx 3.23\%$$
* **Absence of Geometric Bias**: With validation accuracy bounded at chance ($1.40\% \pm 0.36\%$), the flat spectrum verifies that PLVF does not impose spurious geometric structure onto unstructured random data.

## Known Constraints & Scope

1. **Initial Basin Sensitivity & Multi-Seed Variance in Non-Abelian Groups**: While abelian cyclic groups ($\mathbb{Z}_{97}$) exhibit uniform convergence across seeds ($100.00\% \pm 0.00\%$ at step $\sim 2,166$), non-abelian settings ($S_5$) show significant variation in convergence trajectories depending on the initial random seed (ranging from step 3,000 to step 9,000, and up to $> 15,000$ steps). Investigating the geometry of attraction basins and the landscape of Lie-algebraic generators across initializations is an active research direction.
2. **Temporal Low-Pass Characteristic**: The Volterra integral operator utilizes orthogonal Chebyshev polynomials combined with exponential relaxation kernels. Consequently, it acts as a smooth low-pass filter over the temporal axis. Tasks requiring point-wise, discontinuous index jumps (such as sequential bitwise parity evaluated token-by-token) require dense vector formulations to avoid high-frequency signal attenuation.
3. **Metric Parameter Optimization Sensitivity**: Scalar parameters governing Riemannian geometry and thermal bounds ($\tau, \gamma_m, \lambda_m, \text{tangent\_scale}$) must be excluded from standard Euclidean weight decay ($L_2$ regularization). Penalizing metric scale parameters artificially freezes manifold curvature and impedes rotational updates.
4. **Fixed Quadrature Horizon**: The causal Chebyshev basis is evaluated over a fixed sequence horizon ($T$). Variable-length sequence inference requires runtime resampling of the polynomial quadrature grid.
